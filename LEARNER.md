# The reader

Read this before writing an explainer. It is the "customized to me" part of the handbook.
Keep it short and true: add a line when a session teaches you something durable about how
the reader learns (what clicked, what didn't, what they asked you to stop doing), and delete
lines that turn out to be wrong. Date each addition.

## Background

- Works in energy-system planning: capacity expansion, dispatch modelling, PyPSA, MILP optimization.
- Comfortable with code and with reading maths; wants to *understand* mechanisms, not collect definitions.

## What works

- Geometric or visual intuition first, formalism second.
- An interactive where manipulating something reveals the idea — the reader should discover it, not be told it.
- Energy-system examples when they are genuinely illustrative. Not forced: use the topic's most natural example otherwise.
- Common misconceptions named and taken on directly.
- Direct, confident prose. No throat-clearing, no hedging, no being praised for learning.

## What doesn't

- Generic explainer aesthetics and tone ("Let's dive in!", gradient cards, emoji headers).
- Display-mode maths where a plain sentence would do.
- Summaries posing as conclusions.
